# Sources for reference-writing

Checked on their pages on 2026-11-11. Diátaxis is paraphrased: it states
no open licence.

- Diátaxis, [reference](https://diataxis.fr/reference/), paraphrased:
  reference is information for a reader at work, who looks things up
  rather than reading through. It should be neutral and austere, follow
  standard patterns, mirror the structure of what it describes, and may
  describe how the thing behaves and how it is correctly used. Examples
  may illustrate, without turning into instruction.
- [arc42](https://arc42.org/overview): "Twelve sections, each with a
  clear purpose, tailorable to your specific needs", including
  building blocks and decisions; a source for listing parts and
  linking decisions.
- W3C WAI, [complex images](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/complex/):
  a complex image needs "a textual representation of the essential
  information conveyed by the image", hence a sentence after each
  diagram.
- Nielsen Norman Group, [how users read on the
  web](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/):
  "79 percent of our test users always scanned any new page they came
  across"; hence fixed sections and short pages.
- The section list, the contract section and the C4 shape are
  daemorgue's own (decision-15 and decision-16 on its board).
